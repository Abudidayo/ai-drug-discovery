# Fix the seeds, fuse signals, trust one juror

The best-supported gains for this pipeline come from cheap changes to its inputs and its voting rule, not from bigger models. On the retrieval side, published network-medicine results put closest-distance proximity at an **AUROC of about 0.63-0.68 on held-out or time-split indications**, so the current Recall@15 of 5-7% (6-9 times the 0.77% random rate) is normal for the method, not a sign that it is broken. The largest evidence-backed levers are three. First, rebuild disease seeds from genetics-only Open Targets evidence, since genetically supported mechanisms are **2.6 times more likely to reach approval**. Second, add "drugs already approved for neighbouring diseases" and direct target-overlap scores. Third, fuse these with proximity and random walk with restart (RWR) by rank fusion rather than plain averaging. On the reranking side, the 3-model jury did not fail on judgment. It failed on mechanics and dilution: in the blind replication a panel only counted if all three jurors returned a valid ballot, Opus refusals left 5 of 14 panels with no jury ranking, and the literature consistently finds that mixing weaker models with a strong one lowers group quality. The fix with the most support is to run the best single model (GPT-5.4) over 5 shuffled candidate orders and aggregate those runs (permutation self-consistency), with refusals treated as missing ballots. Heavier methods such as TxGNN, knowledge-graph embeddings and LINCS signature reversal either leak the answer, need a GPU, or barely beat a popularity baseline once diseases are truly held out, so they are not worth the day. One caveat dominates everything else: at 14 panels a paired test has only about **12-25% power to detect a 0.10 MRR gain**, so any improvement must be tested on 40-100 panels with a pre-registered, disease-clustered analysis or it will be invisible.

## Proximity tops out near AUROC 0.65, so 5-7% Recall@15 is normal

Every honest re-benchmark of network proximity lands in the same modest range. Re-running Guney's original benchmark (402 known drug-disease pairs against 18,162 unknowns on a curated 13k-protein interactome), Guala and Sonnhammer measured **AUROC 0.677**, falling to **0.633 on a time-stamped split** of drugs that later gained an indication ([Guala and Sonnhammer 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/genetics/articles/10.3389/fgene.2022.792090/full)). In the COVID-19 network-medicine framework, the best proximity pipeline scored AUC 0.63 against an experimental VeroE6 screen and recovered **7 of 77 positives in its top 100 of 6,340 drugs (9%)**, with the best AI pipeline at 11.7% ([Morselli Gysi et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8126852)). The multiscale interactome, which walks over proteins plus a Gene Ontology layer, improved on proximity only from AUROC 0.620 to 0.674 and from Recall@50 0.264 to 0.317 ([Ruiz et al. 2021 supplement](https://zitniklab.hms.harvard.edu/publications/papers/multiscaleinteractomes-ncomms21-supp.pdf)). Our pipeline's top-15 of 1,956 is in the same order of magnitude as Morselli Gysi's top-100 of 6,340, so it sits in line with the literature rather than below it.

The much higher numbers seen elsewhere are mostly artefacts of easy evaluation. A 2025 benchmark of ten heterogeneous-network repurposing methods found per-disease **AUPR below 0.24 for all of them**, against AUPR above 0.93 reported for the same datasets under balanced subsampling ([Nguyen et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12804776/)). KG-Bench, a temporal benchmark built on successive Open Targets releases, reports AUC around 0.91 at one negative per positive but a best **F1 of only 0.28 at 1:100** ([KG-Bench, Bioinformatics 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13171177/)). The clearest warning is Rephetio: a prior based only on how many treatments each node has scored **AUROC 97.9% on known treatments but 54.1% on new DrugCentral indications** ([Himmelstein et al. 2017](https://elifesciences.org/articles/26726)), and across 20 biomedical networks such degree-only priors reach AUROC of at least 0.95 in 17 edge types ([Zietz et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10848215/)).

Popularity matters for interpreting the team's own results. Raw closest distance beat the degree-corrected z-score on 11 diseases (MRR 0.514 against 0.276), and a direct-evidence baseline beat the z-score on Recall@20 (0.217 against 0.145) ([internal RWR comparison](../.trail/learnings/2026-10-25-rwr-functional-comparison-zaid.md)). Both results fit a world where well-studied hub targets genuinely predict membership in a retrospective approval list: Minikel and colleagues found that just **42 targets account for 39% of launched target-indication pairs** ([Minikel et al. 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11096124/fullTextXML)). That signal is real retrospectively and partly an artefact prospectively, so the pipeline should report both raw and z-scored proximity alongside an explicit popularity arm rather than tune the correction away. RWR's edge over the z-score on the official 14 diseases (macro Recall@15 0.069 against 0.051, paired interval −0.006 to +0.044) is not established ([internal shortlist run](../.trail/learnings/2026-10-25-official-14-shortlist-execution-zaid.md)). Extrapolating from these numbers, a realistic target after the fixes below is Recall@15 of roughly 8-15%; no published leakage-controlled benchmark reports Recall@15 for this setup, so that range is an inference.

## Better seeds and borrowed labels beat bigger models at retrieval

### Genetic evidence is the best-validated prior, and it removes leakage too

The most robust finding in the target-to-approval literature is that human genetic support predicts success. Minikel et al. report that genetically supported drug mechanisms have **2.6 times the probability of success**, rising to **3.7 times for OMIM** Mendelian evidence, with every genetic source above 2 times, and the effect grows with confidence in the causal gene rather than with effect size ([Minikel et al. 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11096124/fullTextXML)). King and colleagues found a more than two-fold effect when causal genes are clear, holding prospectively for Mendelian evidence ([King et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/6907751/)). A 2026 preprint found that literature mining alone carried most of a target-approval classifier's performance, which its authors read as temporal leakage, while genetic support held at **OR 3.25 for post-2015 approvals** ([arXiv 2606.14823](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14823)). Propagating L2G-weighted GWAS seeds through a network still recovered ChEMBL phase II+ targets at average AUC above 0.7 with seed genes removed to avoid circularity ([Barrio-Hernandez et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10011132/)), and network-expanded proxies of UK Biobank GWAS hits were enriched for successful targets ([MacNamara et al. 2020](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7708424/)).

The pipeline currently seeds each disease with a fixed top 50 of Open Targets "evidence-only" associations. That mixes literature, animal-model and expression evidence, and a fixed count forces weak genes into rare-gene diseases while truncating well-studied ones; Sadegh and colleagues warn that phenotype-defined association data give network medicine "a blurred view on disease mechanisms" ([Sadegh et al. 2023](https://doaj.org/article/f94efe6e0688493fa30f8c9113b32eb9)). The classic proximity papers instead used curated OMIM and genome-wide-significant GWAS genes with a minimum of 10-20 per disease ([Guney et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4740350/); [Cheng et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6043492/)). The change is to seed from Open Targets genetic datatypes only (GWAS credible sets, gene burden, ClinVar/EVA, Genomics England, Orphanet, Gene2Phenotype, plus somatic evidence for cancers), keep every gene above a score threshold, and use the genetic score as the RWR restart weight instead of a uniform 1/50. Leakage control comes for free: the Open Targets `clinical_precedence` source is built by joining clinical reports to drug mechanisms ([Open Targets evidence docs](https://platform-docs.opentargets.org/evidence)), and the team already showed that JAK2's COVID-19 association came only from clinical precedence and Europe PMC, with baricitinib falling to 22nd once that evidence was zeroed ([internal leakage check](../.trail/learnings/2026-10-24-open-targets-known-answer-checks-leak-jessica.md)).

A direct overlap term should sit beside the network score. In Guney's data, **15.4% of known pairs had a drug target that was itself a disease gene, against 2.7% of unknown pairs (OR 6.6)**, a stronger per-pair signal than proximity though it covers only a minority of pairs ([Guney et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4740350/)). The steelman against leaning on genetics is real: many approved drugs are symptomatic or palliative and act on non-genetic targets, and the OREVID preprint reports that a drug acting on a disease gene with no other evidence was right only **1.8-3.4% of the time** ([OREVID preprint](https://www.preprints.org/frontend/manuscript/6395e853ccac5f68dbff15fb3dcef39f/download_pub)). Genetics is a good prior, not a complete ranker, which is why it belongs inside a fusion rather than replacing proximity.

### Drugs for neighbouring diseases are the strongest cheap signal

"Drugs approved for diseases like this one" consistently outperforms elaborate models. In Rephetio, the best metapaths ran through similar-drug and treats edges, delivering delta AUROC of about 22-23% over permuted networks ([Himmelstein et al. 2017](https://elifesciences.org/articles/26726)). In DREAMwalk, ontology-based semantic similarity was "the largest performance enhancement", lifting AUROC from 0.905 without semantic teleport to 0.944 ([Bang et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10272215/)). OREVID, a preprint that retrained TxGNN with its official code and reproduced its published zero-shot Recall@100 (0.412 against 0.405), attributes most of its own gain to related-disease transfer plus a LightGBM LambdaRank ranker trained with each disease's own treatments masked ([OREVID GitHub](https://github.com/orevid-org/orevid)).

Source: [OREVID GitHub README](https://github.com/orevid-org/orevid). These figures are from a single-author preprint posted in October 2026, a search snippet of a later version gives slightly different external-test numbers, and OREVID feeds Open Targets clinical stages into its features, which is a possible leakage path in its own external test. Two lessons survive those caveats. Popularity alone is a baseline that sophisticated models often barely clear, and transfer from related diseases carries real signal. For this pipeline, transfer means scoring each drug by the summed similarity of other diseases it is approved for, using MONDO/EFO ontology proximity or overlap of genetic seed sets, while excluding the target disease, its synonyms, ancestors and descendants (Open Targets propagates evidence upward through the ontology). One more caution from TxGNN's authors: transfer rewards drugs similar to existing treatments, a shortcut for recovering approvals that is less useful for genuinely new uses ([Huang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11645266/)).

### Fusion beats any single score, but only with the right rule

Across 12 COVID-19 pipelines, "no single predictive algorithm" was consistently reliable: proximity won on the cell screen and AI pipelines won on clinical-trial drugs. Among aggregators, **plain average rank was worst**, Borda was strong on one ground truth but not the other, and CRank was consistently good ([Morselli Gysi et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8126852)). In biomedical reranker fusion, reciprocal rank fusion (RRF) beat weighted score sums because it ignores mismatched score scales ([BIT.UA BioASQ, arXiv 2609.04999](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04999)). RRF over proximity, RWR, direct genetic overlap, disease-similarity transfer and a popularity prior needs no training and gives every drug a score even when it has no network-retained targets. A LambdaRank model trained with leave-one-disease-out folds is the second step if time allows; the transfer and popularity features must then be recomputed inside each fold from training labels only, or they leak the test label.

### Interactome and diffusion tweaks are worth a few points at most

The evidence on which network to use conflicts. Guney found a curated literature-integrated interactome captured therapeutic effects better than STRING ([Guney et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4740350/)), while large disease-gene benchmarks found bigger composite networks such as STRING, HumanNet and FunCoup performed best and performance tracked interaction count ([Wright et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11697402); [Huang et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5920724)). The tasks differ, and no study tests a STRING physical filter like ours for drug ranking. The team's own swap to functional STRING at confidence 700 raised candidate coverage from 66.8% to 70.7% and RWR Recall@20 from 0.211 to 0.229, with a paired interval including zero ([internal RWR comparison](../.trail/learnings/2026-10-25-rwr-functional-comparison-zaid.md)). Coverage is the more concrete problem: **649 of 1,956 drugs have no retained targets, and 64 of 293 reference pairs are unscorable**, which caps network-only Recall at about 78% before any ranking happens ([internal shortlist run](../.trail/learnings/2026-10-25-official-14-shortlist-execution-zaid.md)).

On diffusion, RWR stationary mass scales with node degree. The standard corrections are to score each drug as a percentile against about 1,000 equal-size random seed or target sets, as Barrio-Hernandez did with degree-preserving controls ([Barrio-Hernandez et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10011132/)), or to adjust by eigenvector centrality ([Erten et al. 2011](https://biodatamining.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1756-0381-4-19)). Disease-module methods are a weak bet: apart from DOMINO, active-module tools found no more meaningful modules on real networks than on degree-preserving random ones ([Lazareva et al. 2021](https://cris.fau.de/publications/260103866)). A Parkinson's study reported Recall@50 rising from 0.27 to 0.64 with pathway-weighted edges, but that is one disease and likely tuned in-sample ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378116304_Drug_repurposing_for_Parkinson's_disease_by_biological_pathway_based_edge-weighted_network_proximity_analysis)). Restricting drugs to primary ChEMBL mechanism targets, with bioactivity expansion as an ablation, is worth one run because looser target sets give promiscuous drugs more chances at a short path; Cheng used a looser 10 µM cutoff, while Minikel found single-target restriction gave "very similar results" ([Cheng et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6043492/); [Minikel et al. 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11096124/fullTextXML)).

### Heavy machine learning does not survive a disease-held-out test on a laptop

TxGNN reports a 49.2% indication gain over eight baselines, but these are relative AUPRC gains against sampled negatives on PrimeKG's own DrugBank labels ([Huang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11645266/)). Its public Dataverse record holds only knowledge-graph files and **no drug-disease prediction matrix** ([Harvard Dataverse](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/api/datasets/:persistentId/?persistentId=doi:10.7910/DVN/IXA7BM)). Its code needs DGL 0.5.2 built against CUDA, and OREVID reports retraining across splits took about 22 GPU hours ([TxGNN GitHub](https://github.com/mims-harvard/TxGNN); [OREVID GitHub](https://github.com/orevid-org/orevid)). The `full_graph` model has also seen our test diseases' indication edges. Knowledge-graph embeddings collapse under cold-start: RESCAL's indication MRR falls from 0.27 on a random split to 0.09 on a disease cold-start split and to **0.02 on a degree-preserving permutation**, with the best Orphanet result at MRR 0.016 ([Brière et al., bioRxiv 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.01.23.634511v3.full)); pretrained DRKG embeddings include DrugBank and Hetionet "treats" edges ([DRKG GitHub](https://github.com/gnn4dr/DRKG)). Transcriptomic reversal is weaker still: querying CMap 2 with CMap 1 signatures recovered the same compounds only **17% of the time** ([Lim and Pavlidis 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-97005-z)), and in oncology connectivity scores explained a median 1.6% of drug-sensitivity variance, vanishing after viability correction ([Koudijs et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9851289)).

## The jury lost on mechanics and dilution, not on judgment

The team's own receipts show what went wrong. In the pre-registered blind replication on 14 new diseases, **GPT-5.4 reached MRR 0.461 (Holm p 0.013) and Claude Opus 0.422 (Holm p 0.049)**, Kimi reached 0.374 (p 0.19), and the jury scored 0.210, below the 0.221 chance level. The cause was a rule: a jury ranking only counted when all three jurors were valid, and Opus returned the provider's refusal stop reason on 11 attempts, leaving 5 of 14 panels without a ballot, each scored as a miss ([internal replication learning](../.trail/learnings/2026-10-25-experiment-b-replication-gpt-5-4-jessica.md)). A later audit confirmed the refusals came from the provider's biological-safety classifier on benign public-data packets, with no identifiable trigger ([jury reliability audit](../results/jury_reliability/notes.md)). The newly chosen jury swaps Opus for GLM-5.3, but in the six-panel bake-off GLM and Kimi each produced only 5 of 6 valid ballots because of validator failures, and every model, GPT-5.4 included, scored Hits@5 of 16.7% on those panels, below the 33.3% chance rate ([juror bake-off](../results/juror_bakeoff/summary.md)). Those six were selection panels and too few to rank models, but they show GLM and Kimi are not obviously stronger than GPT-5.4. Notably, the direct-evidence and network baselines scored only 0.111 and 0.182 in the replication, because half the decoys act on disease targets by design; whatever signal the single models extract goes beyond shared-target overlap.

The literature predicts a jury of uneven members will underperform its best member. Self-MoA, which aggregates samples from only the best model, beat standard mixed-model mixture-of-agents by **6.6% on AlpacaEval 2.0 and 3.8% on average** across MMLU, CRUX and MATH, and its authors found that mixing different models "often lowers the average quality" ([Li et al., TMLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00674)). Majority voting explains most of the gain credited to multi-agent debate ([Choi et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.17536)), and more votes can hurt on hard queries ([Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02419v2)). Preprints add that correlated errors set a floor more members cannot remove ([arXiv 2602.08003](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08003)). The strongest counterpoint is PoLL, where a panel of smaller models from different families beat a single GPT-4 judge at grading answers and cost about 7 times less ([Verga et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796v2)). That result concerns grading with balanced members, not ranking with one dominant model and one that refuses, so it argues for keeping a multi-model jury only if members are first shown to be comparably reliable on held-out panels.

Permutation self-consistency is the cheapest well-supported upgrade. It shuffles the candidate order, reruns the ranking, and aggregates. In the original study, **5 shuffles captured about 67% of the gain from 20**, returns diminished after 5-10, temperature made little difference, and single-call GPT-4 passage reranking rose from 60.88 to 64.88 NDCG@10 on TREC DL19; gains were larger for weaker models and harder tasks ([Tang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07712)). Kemeny aggregation beat Borda and RRF by small margins, so the aggregation rule is a second-order choice. The need is real: reordering alone let Vicuna-13B beat ChatGPT on 66 of 80 queries with ChatGPT as judge ([Wang et al., ACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17926)), and reversing the input order cut listwise gpt-3.5 reranking from 65.80 to 32.77 NDCG@10 ([Qin et al., NAACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17563v2)). Pairwise prompting in both orders is nearly immune to order (72.42 to 72.40) but costs 210 calls per 15-item panel, so it suits a diagnostic on a few panels, not the main run. Pointwise rubric scoring ranks less well on passage benchmarks but flips under persuasive distractors only about 9% of the time against 35% for pairwise judges ([Tripathi et al., COLM 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.14716)), which makes it a sensible ablation for evidence packets that contain confident-sounding text.

Evidence content needs pruning rather than expansion. The null result for Reactome pathways fits findings that irrelevant or counterfactual retrieved text hurts biomedical LLMs ([biomedical RAG benchmark, arXiv 2405.08151](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.08151)) and that adding tools or examples can reduce hypothesis-generation quality ([arXiv 2311.05965](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.05965)). K-Paths, by contrast, raised zero-shot repurposing F1 by 4-12 points by feeding a few diverse, disease-specific knowledge-graph paths, though against a baseline with no structured evidence at all ([K-Paths, KDD 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13344)). Replacing broad pathway lists with a handful of diverse disease-to-target paths is therefore plausible but untested. PubMed retrieval is risky: abstracts name drugs and report the very indication being tested, and the published defences are date filters before a known model cutoff ([BioDisco](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.01285)).

Refusals and memorisation need explicit handling in the protocol. Strict refusal rates on identical biology prompts ranged from **0.1% to 94.6% across 19 frontier models** in one preprint ([RefusalBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21545)), and listwise rankers that refuse typically fall back to the input order ([Qin et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.17563v2)); no shared convention exists, so report refusal rate per model and score both an intent-to-treat result (refusal as a miss) and an answered-only result. On memorisation, the team's pilot already measured the gap: named runs reached Hits@5 of 67% while blind runs sat near chance ([internal pilot learning](../.trail/learnings/2026-10-24-jury-pilot-models-beat-chance-only-jessica.md)), mirroring a misinformation study where masking entities cut supported-claim recall from 0.42 to 0.13 while overall accuracy moved 0.03 ([arXiv 2610.04888](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04888)). No published benchmark masks drug names in LLM repurposing ranking, so the blind design is already stricter than the field. Name masking may still leak through distinctive target fingerprints, which is why a subset of approvals dated after each juror's training cutoff is the only direct memorisation control ([KG-Bench temporal design](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13171177/)).

## Fourteen panels cannot see a 0.10 MRR gain; eighty can

The research notes include a simulation (true drug's score drawn as N(d, 1) against 14 N(0, 1) decoys, paired t-test as a proxy for the randomisation test, 800 replicates), which reproduces the stated chance levels of MRR 0.221 and Hits@5 0.333. It is the authors' own calculation under a Gaussian model, not a published table, and real rank distributions may be more bimodal.

| MRR change | Correlation between methods | n = 14 | n = 30 | n = 50 | n = 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.30 to 0.40 | 0.5 | 0.14 | 0.31 | 0.48 | 0.68 |
| 0.30 to 0.40 | 0.8 | 0.25 | 0.49 | 0.74 | 0.89 |
| 0.30 to 0.45 | 0.5 | 0.30 | 0.59 | 0.81 | 0.96 |
| 0.30 to 0.50 | 0.5 | 0.44 | 0.82 | 0.96 | 1.0 |

Power at alpha 0.05; Holm correction across several arms lowers it further. The practical fix is to build one panel per approved drug-disease pair rather than per disease, which yields roughly 40-100 panels from 14-30 diseases, and to analyse with a disease-clustered bootstrap and a paired sign-flip randomisation test on per-disease mean differences. Information-retrieval studies support randomisation and t-tests over Wilcoxon and sign tests, especially with few topics ([Smucker et al.](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/getpdf.php?id=744); [Urbano et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.11096)). For retrieval arms, full-library Recall@15 and MRR over all 1,956 drugs are free to compute and give a more powerful continuous outcome than panels.

Decoy design decides what a panel measures. Matching decoys only on target count leaves a popularity shortcut, because degree-only predictors reach AUROC 0.95 when negatives are not degree-matched ([Zietz et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10848215/)); adding indication count or publication count as matching strata, and running a popularity-only arm on the panels, tests whether the jury is just picking the famous drug. Disease definitions need the same care. KG-Bench drops the EFO measurement branch and non-disease ontologies and keeps the most specific disease level ([KG-Bench](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13171177/)), and improper indication normalisation is a documented source of inflated performance ([Kim et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v209/kim23a.html)). The frozen 14-disease input already contains one such case, EFO_0803536 "multiple sclerosis symptom measurement" in place of multiple sclerosis ([internal shortlist run](../.trail/learnings/2026-10-25-official-14-shortlist-execution-zaid.md)). Finally, the confirmatory diseases must be untouched: the pilot, bake-off, Experiment B and replication diseases are all development data now.

## Five changes to build and test in a day, ranked

| 1 | Single-model permutation self-consistency (GPT-5.4, 5 shuffles, Borda or Kemeny), with any multi-model jury aggregating valid ballots only | Rerank | Panel MRR vs single-call GPT-5.4 | Strong for order bias and Self-MoA; untested on biomedical packets | Low code; 5x calls |
| 2 | Genetics-only, threshold-based disease seeds with clinical, biomarker and literature sources zeroed; genetic score as restart weight | Retrieve | Macro Recall@15 vs current top-50 seeds | Strong that genetics predicts approval; no direct proximity Recall@k test | Low |
| 3 | RRF fusion of proximity, RWR, direct genetic overlap, disease-similarity transfer and popularity | Retrieve | Macro Recall@15 vs RWR alone | Moderate (Morselli Gysi, Rephetio; OREVID preprint) | Low to medium |
| 4 | Coverage rescue for the 649 target-less drugs (mechanism-target fallback, functional STRING union), with fusion scoring the rest | Retrieve | Reference coverage, then Recall@15 | Arithmetic ceiling is certain; ranking gain unproven | Low |
| 5 | Degree-calibrated RWR (percentile against random target sets) plus a primary-mechanism-targets ablation | Retrieve | Recall@15 vs uncalibrated RWR | Weak to moderate; no head-to-head found | Medium compute |

Permutation self-consistency ranks first because it directly addresses the documented failure, the evidence for order bias spans several peer-reviewed venues, and it can run on the existing packets. The pre-registration should fix the shuffle seeds, the number of shuffles, the aggregation rule, the refusal rule (missing ballot, with intent-to-treat and answered-only scores both reported), and a named-versus-blind gap on a subset. If the team wants to keep three companies on the jury for diversity, the defensible version weights or drops members by reliability on held-out pilot panels; with so few panels, a keep-or-drop rule is safer than estimated weights.

Genetics-only seeds rank second because they serve both accuracy and leakage control. The seed change alters the denominator of scorable references, so the protocol must report coverage alongside recall, and it should include a sensitivity run that keeps Europe PMC, since literature co-mentions are a judgment call that has only been shown to leak for COVID-19 × JAK2. Fusion ranks third because its strongest input, disease-similarity transfer, is the most leakage-prone, and the only head-to-head evidence for its size of gain is a preprint; pre-register the exclusion of the target disease's ontology ancestors and descendants, and report a "hard" variant that also drops near-identical siblings. Coverage rescue and degree calibration are cheap, but their expected gains are small and partly overlap with fusion, which already scores drugs that lack network targets.

The protocol wrapper applies to all five: freeze inputs and code hashes before any live call, evaluate on diseases never used in development, build per-pair panels with popularity-matched decoys, keep a popularity arm and a degree-preserving permutation arm, use the disease-clustered sign-flip test with Holm correction, and add a descriptive post-cutoff approvals subset whose cutoff falls after every juror's stated training date. Those training cutoffs were not confirmed for GPT-5.4, GLM-5.3 or Kimi K3 in this research round and must be checked against provider documentation first.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the diagnosis. The retrieval stage is performing at the level the method supports, so further gains come from feeding it cleaner disease biology and letting it borrow what other diseases already know, not from swapping in a graph neural network that would mostly rediscover drug popularity. The jury's failure says less about whether LLMs can reason over evidence than about how votes were counted: the single-model blind results, significant in a pre-registered replication where the on-target baselines sat at chance, are the strongest evidence the pipeline has that its evidence packets carry signal beyond shared targets.

The deeper implication is that this project's binding constraint is statistical, not algorithmic. Every candidate improvement here has an expected effect around 0.05-0.15 MRR or a few points of Recall@15, which 14 panels cannot resolve, so the most valuable work in the day is the evaluation build: per-pair panels, popularity-matched decoys, untouched diseases and a post-cutoff subset. With that in place, even a null result on the five changes becomes informative, and a positive one becomes defensible.
