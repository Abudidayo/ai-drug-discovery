## The circular known-answer check is the one thing that must change

Your `docs/mvp-spec.md` commits to "the COVID-19/baricitinib known-answer check" and promises to "verify whether the evidence pipeline surfaces the candidate; never insert it". That discipline is right, and the pipeline does surface it — Abudi's test bench already confirms it with no help. The problem is what surfacing proves. Open Targets' `disease(efoId:"MONDO_0100096").drugAndClinicalCandidates` contains baricitinib with **22 clinical reports** including ACTT-2, RECOVERY and COV-BARRIER; ChEMBL independently lists COVID-19 as an indication with **17 NCT identifiers**. The contamination runs deeper than the drug record: **COVID-19 × JAK2 scores 0.419 made up only of `clinical_precedence` 0.676 and `europepmc` 0.266** — no genetics, no pathway evidence. A top rank is retrieval of a fact already in the database, and every LLM juror has known the baricitinib story since the Lancet letter of February 2020 ([Richardson et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(20)30304-4)). Two leakage channels, both wide open.

Filtering out the disease's own drugs does not fix it. That removes the drug but leaves `clinical_precedence` inflating the target score, which is where the real leak lives. The correct cut is to zero the datasource — and the enabling discovery from the live research is that **you can do this entirely client-side with numbers that match the API exactly**. An Open Targets overall score is the harmonic sum of weighted datasource scores divided by π²/6, and that formula was reproduced client-side for **all 8,235 COVID rows and all 5,341 multiple myeloma rows with maximum absolute error 0.0000**. One bulk fetch (COVID: 3 pages, ~6.1 s; myeloma: 2 pages, ~4.1 s, page size capped at 3000) buys you arbitrary re-weighting, datasource ablation and date cut-offs, all offline and all exact. There is a server-side route via the `datasources` argument, but it carries a trap: the argument **replaces the whole weight table rather than patching it**, so passing only `clinical_precedence: 0.0` silently promotes every other source to 1.0 and gives TYK2 rank 5 instead of rank 2.

Run the ablation and the story gets better, not worse. Under the default scheme TYK2 is rank 1 and JAK1 rank 25; drop `clinical`, and **TYK2 holds rank 2 while JAK2 collapses from 96 to 2043**. Fan out from the top-100 no-clinical targets and 60 distinct parent drugs appear, but baricitinib sits at rank 22, reached only through JAK1 — because **Open Targets' curated mechanism of action for baricitinib is JAK1 and JAK2 only** (from PubMed 20363976), so it never touches the top genetic target. Add ChEMBL bioactivity edges (human targets, median pChEMBL ≥ 7) and baricitinib gains AAK1, BMP2K, SEPTIN9, STAT3 and **TYK2**, moving to **joint first** with tofacitinib, ruxolitinib, upadacitinib, filgotinib, delgocitinib and momelotinib. A 2020 time cut-off survives too: baricitinib's TYK2 potency is published at **IC50 61 nM (pChEMBL 7.21) in 2014**, and genetics as of 31 December 2020 put **TYK2 at rank 5** off the COVID-19 Host Genetics Initiative GWAS, with JAK1 absent entirely. The underlying science agrees — Pairo-Castineira et al. found the TYK2 signal at rs74956615 and wrote that "high expression of TYK2" is associated with life-threatening disease and "may be amenable to targeted treatment with existing drugs" ([Nature 2020, PMID 33307546](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-03065-y)).

**Demo this as two passes, and make the contrast the product.** Pass 1 with all evidence, labelled plainly as leaky retrieval. Pass 2 in "evidence-only mode" with clinical weight zeroed and ChEMBL-augmented edges, where the pipeline rediscovers baricitinib through a target its own curated mechanism never mentions. A model jury that agrees unanimously in pass 1 and argues in pass 2 is a far stronger artefact than one ranked list. Build a `leak_flags` field per candidate (`reached_via_clinical`, `named_in_cancer_biomarkers`, `already_indicated_for_disease`) and surface it — it converts the project's main scientific weakness into its headline feature.

Multiple myeloma needs the same treatment and one extra cut. Its default top 25 is **even more circular**: six of them are proteasome subunits whose association is ~99% `clinical_precedence` (`PSMB5 = clinical_precedence 0.99 + europepmc 0.15`). Oncology also has a second leak channel — **`cancer_biomarkers` names the drug inside the evidence row** (MM × BCL2 rows literally read "BCL2 overexpression / VENETOCLAX"). Drop both and the ranking becomes genuine cancer genomics: TP53, LIG4, KRAS, NRAS, FGFR3, BRAF. **Venetoclax via BCL2 survives the strictest cut** (clinical, cancer_biomarkers and literature all dropped; BCL2 still rank 55, venetoclax 24 of 88 approved drugs reached), and **erdafitinib via FGFR3 at rank 4** is the "new idea" with real t(4;14) biology behind it. Both are small molecules with good structures — most of myeloma's actual top drugs are antibodies, ADCs and CAR-Ts that cannot be docked at all, so **gate the docking button on `drugType == "Small molecule"` plus a resolvable PubChem CID**.

## Architecture: one A40 in London, Cortex for the jury, Tiger for time series

Keep the shape simple and deterministic. A FastAPI (or Node) backend on a small Vultr VM in London holds the pipeline; the **LLM never writes GraphQL, SQL or Cypher** — AstraZeneca's production team documented exactly why, reporting that LLM-generated graph queries make behaviour "non-deterministic", miss meta-level distinctions ("one gene-disease association edge may reflect a genuine causal impact while another only a change in expression level"), and "can be factually correct, but too complex and lead to timeouts" ([arXiv:2608.12395](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.12395v1#S2.SS3.SSS3)). Pin the four verified queries into the repo and let the model edit them, never author them.

The docking job must not live inside a request. The robust minimum: `POST /api/dock` computes a cache key = SHA-256 of (pdb_id, chain, canonical SMILES, box centre/size, engine, engine version, exhaustiveness, seed), inserts a row with `status='queued'`, returns `202` + job id, and returns instantly if the key already exists. A single worker polls with `SELECT ... WHERE status='queued' ORDER BY created_at FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED LIMIT 1` — a correct queue in Postgres with no Redis and no broker to debug at 4 a.m. The React app polls every 2 s rather than using SSE, because polling never breaks behind conference Wi-Fi. Enforce a hard 180 s timeout and a per-session cap, and write `receptor.pdb`, `poses.sdf`, `scores.json` and `log.txt` to object storage with URLs in the row — **never blobs in Postgres**.

## The 26.09 field names will break every LLM-generated query you write

This is the highest-probability technical failure in the next 21 hours, and it is entirely avoidable. Open Targets 26.09 (API `26.9.0`, data published 2026-09-18) **renamed the entire known-drug surface**, and introspection confirms that **no field or enum value in the whole schema carries `isDeprecated: true`**. Renames are hard breaks: any tutorial, blog post or model answering from pre-September-2026 training data will emit `knownDrugs { rows { drug { ... } } }` and get an "undefined field" error rather than a warning. Abudi's test bench already hit this and recorded it as a learning. Pin these into the repo:

| Dead field (pre-2024 tutorials) | Live 26.09 replacement |
|---|---|
| `Disease.knownDrugs` | `Disease.drugAndClinicalCandidates { count rows { id maxClinicalStage drug {...} clinicalReports {...} } }` |
| `Drug.isApproved`, `Drug.hasBeenWithdrawn` | `Drug.maximumClinicalStage: String!` + `Drug.drugWarnings` |
| `Drug.maximumClinicalTrialPhase` | `Drug.maximumClinicalStage` |
| `Drug.blackBoxWarning` | `Drug.drugWarnings { warningType toxicityClass ... }` |
| `Drug.linkedDiseases`, `Drug.linkedTargets` | `Drug.indications: clinicalIndicationsFromDrugImp!` |
| datatype `known_drug` / datasource `chembl` | datatype `clinical` / datasource `clinical_precedence` |

Five more live-verified traps that will cost you an hour each if you meet them cold. **`drugAndClinicalCandidates` takes no `size` or `page` argument** and returns everything — 2.86 MB for myeloma; budget for it. **`row.maxClinicalStage` ≠ `drug.maximumClinicalStage`**: the former is the max stage for that target–drug pair (baricitinib×JAK1 is `PHASE_4`), the latter across all indications (`APPROVAL`). **`ClinicalReport.year` was null on every ClinicalTrials.gov report inspected** — use `trialStartDate`. **PubChem silently renamed its output keys**: requesting `CanonicalSMILES` returns a key called `SMILES`, so code keyed on the old name breaks without error. **RCSB returns HTTP 204 with an empty body** on zero hits, not a 200. And `mapIds` beats free-text `search` for normalisation — searching "covid" returns `MONDO_0100233 long COVID-19` as the top hit, while `mapIds` returns `COVID-19 → MONDO_0100096` with score 1 in 107 ms.

Two data-quality facts belong on screen, not buried. **Open Targets records baricitinib × COVID-19 at `PHASE_3`, not `APPROVAL`**, although the FDA approved Olumiant for hospitalised adults on 10 May 2022 — a consequence of the 25.03 note about filtering Phase IV evidence lacking indication-specific approval. Never say "Open Targets knows it's approved for COVID" on stage; cite the regulator's label instead. Second, of the APPROVAL-stage drug records reached from top-100 no-clinical targets, **myeloma had 110 total but only 83 regulator-backed; 27 were weak**, asserted only by ATC or TTD. **Navitoclax has never been approved** and will otherwise appear in front of judges as a drug. Filter on `clinicalReports.source ∈ {FDA, EMA Human Drugs, PMDA, DailyMed}`.

One download is worth the clock: roughly **127 MB of Parquet** (`drug_molecule`, `drug_mechanism_of_action`, `drug_warning`, `clinical_indication`, `clinical_target`, `clinical_report`, `disease`, `target`) plus `chembl_uniprot_mapping.txt` (1.2 MB). Anonymous S3 listing works with no credentials. This replaces the heaviest live calls with local joins and **makes the whole app demo-able offline if the venue Wi-Fi dies** — real insurance for a judged demo. Skip the 2.3–2.6 GB association files and the 5.4 GB ChEMBL SQLite; live calls at 100–400 ms are fine. Note Open Targets' own docs "discourage users from repeatedly querying the GraphQL API one entity at a time", so batch with `targets(ensemblIds: [...])` — verified at 200 ids in 571 ms.

## The evaluation numbers that win the HRT prize

Every HRT "Best Use of Data" winner found across seven hackathons shares four traits: real external data fused from several sources, an explicit quantitative model, a reported validation number, and output that feeds a decision. WildFire at IC Hack 26 used "stochastic Percolation-based simulations and Monte Carlo techniques" over satellite, fire-department, vegetation and weather APIs, and used Claude only "for summarising historical events" ([WildFire](https://devpost.com/software/wildfire-rcz2xb)). Earlier winners reported **92% zero-shot classification accuracy** and **96% detection accuracy** ([Bino-SoRAs](https://devpost.com/software/watchdog-onxyge); [ShipSense](https://devpost.com/software/shipsense-ai-novel-data-augmentation-protocol)). A biomedical research tool has already won it — **STIK GUI, glioblastoma spheroid tumour invasion kinetics, TreeHacks 2025** ([STIK GUI](https://devpost.com/software/tumor-invasion-kinetics-gui)). You are squarely in scope, and the only thing missing from your current plan is the number.

Build it as a small pre-registered retrospective check, borrowing KG-Bench's protocol. **Take 30–50 held-out drug–disease pairs, hide each true drug among 19 popularity-matched approved decoys, mask all direct evidence for the pair, and report Hits@5, Hits@10 and MRR with 1000-resample bootstrap CIs against random and popularity baselines.** Chance for Hits@5 of 20 candidates is 25%; MRR chance is ≈0.18. With n=30, the 95% CI half-width at a ~50% hit rate is about ±0.18, tightening to ±0.14 at n=50 — so **50 beats 30**, and 30 is the floor. Listwise ranking costs 1 call per disease per juror per permutation: 50 × 4 jurors × 2 permutations = **400 calls**, against 4,000 for pointwise scoring. Popularity matching is not optional: KG-Bench's own supplementary data shows positive pairs at **mean degree 61.3 against 13.4–17.3 for random negatives**, which means random-negative AUC partly measures hub popularity rather than biology.

Quote KG-Bench for calibration, and quote the published version only — the preprint's headline differs. **RGCNConv reached AUC 0.91 [0.87–0.94] at a balanced 1:1 split, but average precision collapsed to 0.16–0.21 across all six architectures at 1:100, with TransformerConv's best F1 at 0.28** ([btag159, Bioinformatics 42(5)](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag159)). That is the single most useful context line you can give a judge: even purpose-built graph neural networks on a time-split benchmark reach about 0.2 average precision under realistic imbalance. It frames every ranked drug as a hypothesis and inoculates you against the overclaiming that sinks projects with technical judges. Be honest about KG-Bench's own limits too — its time split is by Open Targets *curation* date, not approval date (edges jump from ~10 per release to ~450 at 2022.04, which looks like a pipeline change), and its disease–target edges use unfiltered overall association scores, so clinical-precedence leakage is a path the paper never discusses.

For docking, ship one number as a first-class feature, not a footnote. **Strip baricitinib out of 6WTO (JAK2 JH1, 1.74 Å, one chain, 309 residues), re-dock it with the same box and settings used for user jobs, and display "Redocking control: baricitinib into 6WTO, RMSD = X.X Å (success threshold 2.0 Å), PoseBusters: PASS"** ([RCSB 6WTO](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/6WTO)). The experimental bound pose is one fetch away at `models.rcsb.org/v1/6WTO/ligand?label_comp_id=3JW&encoding=sdf` (3,160 bytes, 691 ms), and spyrmsd for symmetry-corrected RMSD is already an MIT-licensed DiffDock dependency. Add a negative control — an unrelated ligand into the same box — so the audience sees the viewer happily draw a confident-looking wrong answer. Two structural warnings: **there is no JAK1–baricitinib structure in the PDB** (verified HTTP 204), so say "JAK2, the JAK family member for which a co-crystal exists" rather than implying a JAK1 result; and **avoid 5LF3**, where the 20S proteasome is a 28-mer of 6,458 residues and bortezomib is a covalent adduct that both Vina and DiffDock would model as non-covalent. For myeloma use **6O0K (BCL2 + venetoclax, 1.62 Å, 166 residues)** — the easiest structure in either disease. Finally, **pin and display your RDKit version**: a single RDKit bump halved DiffDock's reported performance from ~51% to ~24% with no model change ([BLOPIG, Apr 2025](https://www.blopig.com/blog/2025/04/featurisation-is-key-one-version-change-that-halved-diffdocks-performance/)). A result that moves 27 points on a dependency bump is a result you version-stamp.

### The jury must be measured, not asserted

Design it as forecast aggregation, because that is the vocabulary quant judges speak. Every juror gets an **identical evidence packet** with candidate order shuffled per juror (seed recorded), temperature 0, and a JSON schema returning rank, a 0–1 score, `supporting_ids`, `contradicting_ids`, an `insufficient_evidence` flag and a ≤40-word rationale in which **every sentence ends with an `[E#]` reference**. A server-side validator rejects any ID not in the packet, re-asks once, then drops that juror and records why — and you display the **citation-validity rate** per run. This directly answers Bayer's Subrata Bose, who told the AI Summit London that LLM tools are "sometimes making up journals which never exist", that he had just seen one give "the right paper title but a different journal's name", and — crucially for you — that he does **not** rely on the output of multiple LLMs alone ([Video 20:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MpUTfZpiePM&t=1222s); [Video 26:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MpUTfZpiePM&t=1594s)). A live demo moment where a juror cites an ID outside the pack and the UI flags it red is memorable, cheap and exactly on this theme.

Aggregate with Borda count or reciprocal-rank fusion; report **Kendall's W across jurors, pairwise τ and top-3 overlap**; flag large-spread candidates as "contested". Add the non-LLM **"juror zero"** — the deterministic harmonic evidence score — as a fourth voice. Disagreement between the LLMs and the deterministic score is a much better visual than LLM-versus-LLM alone, and it directly addresses the "black box" objection. Present agreement as "models concur given this evidence", never as confidence: LLMs trained on overlapping corpora make correlated errors, so five models agreeing is not five pieces of evidence. AstraZeneca's analogous Discover Agent deliberately ensembles **structurally different** graph-ML models (GCN/GAT, RotatE, ComplEx, DWPC, Sørensen, PageRank) combined with CRank, precisely because heterogeneity is what makes agreement informative.

Two measurements make the jury defensible rather than decorative. Run each model **closed-book (no packet) and open-book (packet)** and report how rankings and agreement change — DeepEvidence's own data show the evidence layer matters far more than the model, with GPT-5 scoring 3.3% alone on HLE-Medicine against 40.0% inside the full system ([arXiv:2601.11560](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11560v1)). Then run the **masked + blinded** condition with drug names replaced by "Drug A/B/C". The gap between open and blinded is a visible, honest measure of how much the answer comes from leakage or memory rather than mechanism — a look-ahead-bias-removed backtest that HRT judges will recognise instantly.

## What you can say, what you cannot, and what must be in the repo by noon

Claims you can defend: that every statement links back to the database record it came from, with the citation-validity rate shown; that several independent models ranked identical evidence and here is their Kendall's W; that the pipeline recovers a documented repurposing success; that **with clinical-precedence evidence zeroed and names blinded, baricitinib ranked #k of N** — but only if you actually ran it; that docking shows one computationally predicted pose for illustration; and that the design follows evidence-graph patterns published by the DeepEvidence and AstraZeneca BIKG teams. Claims to refuse: "discovers new treatments", "validated", "predicts efficacy", "X% accurate" from one or two examples, "the jury is more accurate than a single model" without CIs, "hallucination-free" (even AstraZeneca's production system reports persistent hallucinations), "approved for X" sourced from Open Targets rather than a regulator, and any reading of a docking score as binding affinity — DiffDock's own README states the confidence score "is not a binding affinity measure". Ship the disclaimer: *educational research tool; outputs are hypotheses generated from public databases and AI models, can be wrong or incomplete, and are not medical advice.*

Three framing numbers are worth memorising for the pitch, because they place the app honestly in the funnel. When 918 computer-ranked drugs were physically screened against SARS-CoV-2 in cells, **about 8.4% showed any antiviral effect, and 76 of the 77 actives worked through mechanisms docking would have missed entirely** ([Gysi et al. 2021, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2025581118)). Most computational repurposing predictions are **never experimentally tested at all** ([Galindez et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-020-00007-6)). And baricitinib — the exception that makes the field worth doing — went from hypothesis in February 2020 to FDA approval in May 2022, about 27 months, while in the same period all four repurposed antivirals in WHO Solidarity failed ([WHO Solidarity 2021, NEJM](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2023184)). Open the expo pitch on the backtest, not the UI: *"With direct COVID evidence masked, the jury still ranks baricitinib #k of N — and here is where the models disagree."* Then the myeloma run live, then the cached pose with the decision label updating, then agreement metrics and provenance. MLH is explicit that "pitches and presentations are discouraged. You are not judged on the quality of your pitch" — so demo, do not present ([MLH Standard Hackathon Rules](https://github.com/MLH/mlh-policies/blob/main/standard-hackathon-rules.md)).

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding in this research is that your project's biggest scientific weakness and its strongest competitive asset are the same thing. Everyone building a drug-repurposing demo this weekend will show a ranked list; the ones that lose to a quant-firm judge will be the ones that cannot answer "is this prediction or retrieval?". You can answer it with an exact number, because Open Targets' scoring function is reproducible client-side to **0.0000 error**, which means datasource ablation, re-weighting and date cut-offs are a free, offline operation rather than a research project. That single property turns a leaky demo into a backtest, and a backtest is what the HRT prize description — "Best Use of Data for **Predictions** and **Decision Making**" — is literally asking for.

The GPU question resolves cleanly in the same direction. The temptation to rent a B200 is the temptation to spend $130–210 and several hours of dependency archaeology buying latency improvements a human cannot perceive, on hardware where your chosen docking stack cannot load its own kernels. The A40 is not a compromise; it is the correct answer on compatibility grounds, cheaper by a factor of four, available in London, and the only choice that keeps a sponsor prize alive. Spend the hours you save on the thing that cannot be bought: 50 masked pairs, bootstrap confidence intervals, and a juror that gets dropped on screen when it cites a source that does not exist.
